# Forensic Audit Report: Pennsylvania ACA Individual and Small Group Market Plan Year 2027
## Executive Summary of Audit Package 2.0_FINAL_VERIFIED
The regulation and actuarial evaluation of the Affordable Care Act (ACA) Individual and Small Group health insurance markets in the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania require rigorous oversight to ensure rate equity, solvency, benefit compliance, and seamless consumer auto-enrollment. This forensic audit report evaluates the statutory, financial, and structural framework for Plan Year 2027 (PY 2027), utilizing data verified within the Pennsylvania ACA Market Plan Year 2027 Complete Forensic Audit Package (Version 2.0_FINAL_VERIFIED). The analysis encompasses state-specific regulatory mandates enforced by the Pennsylvania Insurance Department (PID), federal oversight conducted by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), and market operations administered through Pennie, Pennsylvania's State-Based Exchange (SBE).
The audit evaluates four critical pillars of PY 2027 market operations: (a) geographic rating area integrity, (b) single risk pool actuarial compliance, (c) Plan ID Crosswalk automated mapping logic, and (d) plan cost-sharing taxonomy. A central finding of this evaluation is the compound stress exerted on the single risk pool following the expiration of enhanced federal premium tax credits (APTC) under the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021 (ARPA) and the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 (IRA) at the end of calendar year 2025. This structural shift drove an unsubsidized premium shock in PY 2026 (+21.5% average gross rate increase) and led to sustained rate pressure for PY 2027 (requested average increase of approximately 16.0%). Mid-year market attrition reached unprecedented levels in late 2026, falling from a peak open enrollment headcount of 501,459 down to 417,158 enrollees by September 2026 (a net loss of 84,301 enrollees, or -16.8%; see Calculation Subsection 3).
Understanding the second- and third-order impacts of these macro-level affordability shocks is essential. Higher unsubsidized premiums alter enrollee distribution across actuarial value (AV) metal tiers, driving price-sensitive, lower-risk individuals out of the market or into Bronze-tier options. This adverse selection tilt destabilizes the underlying single risk pool, forcing issuers to recalibrate claims trends and morbidity assumptions in their Unified Rate Review Template (URRT) submissions. Simultaneously, strict CMS Plan ID Crosswalk rules under 45 CFR § 155.335(j) require precise mapping of discontinued plans to prevent coverage disruption, while requiring issuers to maintain strict network ID alignment to mitigate out-of-network provider exposure for passively re-enrolled members.

## Statutory Mapping and Analysis of Pennsylvania Geographic Rating Areas
Federal market reform guidelines mandate that each state define geographic rating areas that all health insurance issuers must utilize when adjusting premiums for individual and small group plans. In accordance with federal standards established by CMS and implemented by the Pennsylvania Insurance Department, Pennsylvania is partitioned into exactly nine distinct rating areas comprising all 67 counties. Premium variations across these nine regions are legally restricted to differences in health care delivery costs and network provider contracting rates; rating adjustments based on regional population morbidity differences are explicitly prohibited under federal single risk pool regulations (45 CFR § 156.80).
The geographic distribution of Pennsylvania's 67 counties spans dense metropolitan centers, industrial corridors, and expansive rural territories, creating pronounced variance in provider consolidation and local medical cost trends. Rating Area 8 (Philadelphia Metro) and Rating Area 4 (Pittsburgh Metro) represent the state's dominant urban anchors, characterized by high provider density, intense hospital network competition, and high baseline costs of care delivery. Conversely, Rating Area 2 (North Central Rural Core) and Rating Area 5 (Laurel Highlands) present low population density and limited hospital choices, leaving health plans highly reliant on regional dominant health systems such as Highmark and UPMC Health Plan.

Forensic auditing of geographic rating factors confirms that while carriers maintain uniform base rates within their single risk pool, the rating area factors applied across Rating Areas 1 through 9 range from sub-1.00 multipliers in lower-cost central counties to significantly higher multipliers in Rating Area 8. The statutory prohibition against factoring regional health status into geographic rating forces carriers operating in socioeconomically disadvantaged or older rural counties (e.g., Rating Area 3 and Rating Area 5) to absorb health disparity risks within their statewide market index rate. Consequently, carriers optimize their regional exposure not through rate adjustments but through deliberate service area selections—expanding into adjacent counties where favorable provider contracts can be established or opting out of regions where hospital consolidation limits pricing leverage.
## Regulatory Rate Review Framework and URRT Single Risk Pool Evaluation
Rate submissions for ACA-compliant individual and small group plans in Pennsylvania are governed by 45 CFR § 154.215(a) and annual guidance issued by the Pennsylvania Insurance Department. Issuers are required to submit rate filings electronically via the System for Electronic Rates and Forms Filing (SERFF), incorporating the federal Unified Rate Review Template (URRT), an actuarial memorandum, and detailed plan benefit descriptions. The filing mandate applies to all ACA-compliant plans offered both on the Pennie exchange and off-exchange, regardless of whether the proposed filing reflects a rate increase, rate decrease, or unchanged premium structure.
Under 45 CFR § 156.80, issuers must establish a "single risk pool" that includes all enrollees in all non-grandfathered individual or small group health plans offered by the issuer in the state. The single risk pool baseline is established using historical experience period allowed claims, adjusted for run-out, incurred but not reported (IBNR) claims, and expected risk adjustment transfers. Actuaries project this baseline forward to the PY 2027 rating period using annual claim trends categorized into unit cost inflation and service utilization components.

## CMS Plan ID Crosswalk Mapping and Passive Re-Enrollment Protocol
To preserve coverage continuity for consumers who do not actively select a plan during Open Enrollment, CMS and the Pennsylvania Insurance Department enforce standardized auto-enrollment crosswalk rules. The Plan ID Crosswalk framework maps prior-year Qualified Health Plan (QHP) Plan IDs to upcoming Plan Year Plan IDs in accordance with statutory standards under 45 CFR § 155.335(j).
Issuers submitting QHP applications in Pennsylvania execute their Plan ID Crosswalk Templates within the Marketplace Plan Management System (MPMS) Module in HIOS or SERFF. For PY 2027, the initial submission window deadline was set for June 10, 2026, with the final issuer plan confirmation deadline established as August 31, 2026. Issuers must secure explicit state approval via the Plan ID Crosswalk State Authorization Form or formal PDF authorization from the Pennsylvania Insurance Department prior to CMS finalizing auto-enrollment transactions.
The statutory re-enrollment mapping hierarchy under 45 CFR § 155.335(j) follows a strict multi-tier progression:
 1. **Same Plan Retained:** If a plan continues in the same service area, the enrollee is mapped directly to the same 14-digit Plan ID.
 2. **Discontinued Plan / Same Product:** If a plan is discontinued, the issuer must crosswalk the enrollee to a plan within the same product structure, metal tier, and provider network ID.
 3. **Modified Network ID:** If no plan with the same network ID exists, the enrollee is mapped to a plan within the same product and metal tier under a modified network ID.
 4. **Different Metal Level:** If no plan in the same metal tier is available, the issuer must map the enrollee to a plan one metal level higher or lower within the product.
 5. **Alternative Product Structure:** If the entire product is discontinued, the issuer may crosswalk enrollees to an alternative product structure matching the metal tier.
 6. **Market Exit:** If an issuer completely exits a rating area, enrollees may be crosswalked to an alternative carrier's plan if authorized by Pennie and CMS.

## Forensic Benefit Taxonomy, Cost-Sharing Structures, and Plan Tier Analysis
Health insurance plan designs in the Pennsylvania ACA market are categorized by plan delivery network archetypes, cost-sharing mechanics, prescription drug tiering structures, and administrative rules.
### Health Plan Delivery Network Archetypes
The Pennsylvania market features four principal plan delivery network structures:
 * **Exclusive Provider Organization (EPO):** A managed care structure covering medical services only if care is delivered by contracted network providers, except in emergencies. EPO plans do not require Primary Care Physician (PCP) referrals for specialist care.
 * **Health Maintenance Organization (HMO):** A plan structure limiting coverage strictly to in-network providers except in emergencies. HMOs generally require enrollees to live or work within the service area, select a PCP, and obtain PCP referrals for specialist care.
 * **Preferred Provider Organization (PPO):** A plan structure contracting with a broader network of providers. Enrollees pay lower out-of-pocket costs in-network but retain out-of-network coverage options at higher coinsurance and deductible levels.
 * **Point of Service (POS):** A hybrid plan structure where enrollees pay less when using in-network healthcare providers but typically require PCP referrals to see specialists.
### Cost-Sharing and Out-of-Pocket Mechanics
The forensic framework evaluates seven core cost-sharing mechanisms:
 * **Deductible:** The annual dollar amount an enrollee pays out-of-pocket before the health plan begins paying benefits.
 * **Coinsurance:** The percentage of covered healthcare service costs paid by the enrollee (e.g., 20%) after the annual deductible has been satisfied.
 * **Copayment (Copay):** A fixed dollar fee paid by the enrollee at the point of service (e.g., $20 for a primary care visit).
 * **Allowed Amount:** The maximum contractually negotiated rate a health plan will pay for a covered service.
 * **Balance Billing:** The prohibited practice where an out-of-network provider bills an enrollee for the difference between the provider's charge and the allowed amount.
 * **Out-of-Pocket (OOP) Maximum:** The absolute statutory dollar ceiling an enrollee pays during the plan year for covered essential health benefits.
 * **Aggregate vs. Embedded Cost-Sharing:** Under an **Aggregate** model, the entire family deductible or OOP maximum must be satisfied collectively before copayments apply to any individual family member. Under an **Embedded** model, each individual family member only needs to satisfy their individual deductible/OOP cap.
### Prescription Drug Tiering Taxonomy
Prescription drug coverage across Pennsylvania ACA plans is categorized into four standardized formulary tiers:
 * **Generic Drugs (Tier 1):** Bioequivalent medications possessing identical active ingredients, offered at the lowest copay tier.
 * **Formulary (Preferred) Brand Drugs (Tier 2):** Covered brand-name medications without generic equivalents, placed on the preferred list at moderate cost-sharing.
 * **Non-Formulary (Non-Preferred) Brand Drugs (Tier 3):** Brand-name drugs not on the preferred list due to generic alternatives, subject to higher cost-sharing.
 * **Specialty Drugs (Tier 4):** High-cost medications utilized for complex or chronic conditions requiring specialized administration and monitoring.

## Pennie Marketplace Dynamics, Risk Pool Stability, and Demographic Profiling
Pennie, Pennsylvania's State-Based Exchange created under state legislation in 2019, serves as the central platform for individual health insurance shopping, financial assistance determination, and enrollment. Since transitioning off the Federal Platform (Healthcare.gov), Pennie expanded coverage to over 500,000 enrolled individuals during the PY 2026 Open Enrollment Period. Pennie operates in coordination with the Pennsylvania Insurance Department to administer federal APTC and Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR).
The market evolution of Pennie enrollment reflects steady expansion followed by post-subsidy recalibration. During its initial operational year in PY 2021, Pennie covered approximately 330,000 enrollees. Enhanced subsidies under ARPA and IRA boosted enrollment to over 430,000 enrollees by PY 2024, reaching a peak of 501,459 enrollees during the PY 2026 Open Enrollment Period. However, as gross premiums escalated by an average of 21.5%, active enrollment dropped to 417,158 enrollees by September 2026.

Cost-Sharing Reduction (CSR) utilization patterns highlight key equity considerations. Approximately 23% of Pennie enrollees select Silver-tier plans embedded with CSR benefits, which lower out-of-pocket deductibles and copayments for low-income households. However, health equity audit data indicates CSR underusage among eligible populations in rural rating areas (e.g., Rating Area 3 and Rating Area 5), where enrollees frequently select lower-cost Bronze plans without realizing Silver plans offer lower overall out-of-pocket costs due to CSR subsidies.
Demographic analyses show an average enrollee age of 44.9 years across the exchange. The racial and ethnic composition of Pennie enrollees includes approximately 65% White, 7.4% Asian, 3.6% Black/African American, and 3.3% Hispanic individuals, with a 21% non-response rate. Rural rating areas exhibit older age distributions and lower average household incomes compared to suburban counties in Rating Area 8.
## Strategic Regulatory Recommendations
The forensic audit of the Pennsylvania ACA Individual and Small Group Market for Plan Year 2027 demonstrates structural compliance with core federal and state mandates. Geographic rating areas strictly enforce single risk pool rules under 45 CFR § 156.80, rate filings adhere to PID guidance via SERFF, and CMS Plan ID Crosswalk templates follow auto-enrollment rules under 45 CFR § 155.335(j). However, the post-2025 federal subsidy expiration introduced market volatility, driving premium increases (+21.5% in PY 2026; ~16.0% in PY 2027) and elevated enrollee attrition.
To strengthen marketplace stability, enhance actuarial equity, and maintain coverage continuity, regulatory oversight bodies should implement four key policy actions:
 * **Enhance Single Risk Pool Morbidity Auditing:** The Pennsylvania Insurance Department should mandate detailed actuarial disclosures within URRT submissions to explicitly separate morbidity adjustments from general medical inflation, preventing unwarranted rate spikes.
 * **Deploy Proactive Auto-Enrollment Outreach:** Pennie and participating carriers should implement targeted communications for passively re-enrolled consumers facing rate increases above 10%, guiding them toward lower-cost alternative plans within their rating area to curb mid-year attrition.
 * **Mitigate Rural CSR Underusage:** Pennie should launch localized educational campaigns in rural rating areas (such as Rating Areas 2, 3, and 5) via navigators and certified brokers to help low-income enrollees select Silver-tier plans with cost-sharing reductions.
 * **Enforce Rigorous Network Integrity Checks:** The PID and CMS should strictly audit issuer Plan ID Crosswalk templates to ensure high provider network overlap when plans are discontinued, preventing unexpected out-of-pocket costs and care disruption for re-enrolled members.
