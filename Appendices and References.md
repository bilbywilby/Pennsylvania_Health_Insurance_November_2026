## 6. Pennie Marketplace Dynamics, Risk Pool Stability, and Demographic Profiling

Pennie, Pennsylvania’s State-Based Exchange (SBE), serves as the central operational hub for individual marketplace enrollment, eligibility determinations, Advanced Premium Tax Credits (APTC), and Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR). Following its operational launch in Plan Year 2021 (transitioning off the Federal Platform Healthcare.gov), Pennie achieved historic peak coverage during the PY 2026 Open Enrollment Period (OEP), enrolling 501,459 unique individuals [Pennsylvania Insurance Department, 2026](https://www.insurance.pa.gov).

However, the expiration of enhanced APTC caps under the Inflation Reduction Act at the end of calendar year 2025 created a severe affordability shock. By September 2026, mid-year active enrollment contracted to 417,158 enrollees, reflecting an unprecedented mid-year attrition rate of −16.8% (a net loss of 84,301 enrollees; see Appendix A, Calculation Subsection 3).

### 6.1 Actuarial Metal Tier Redistribution and Adverse Selection

The erosion of premium subsidies fundamentally altered enrollee distribution across actuarial metal tiers (Catastrophic, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum). Lower-risk, price-sensitive unsubsidized enrollees migrated downward to High-Deductible Bronze plans or exited the individual market entirely. 

- **Bronze Tier Migration:** Enrollees seeking to minimize monthly premium outlays migrated to Expanded Bronze plans (AV 56%–65%). While providing protection against catastrophic medical expense, these plans carry high deductibles (frequently exceeding $7,500 for single coverage), driving high point-of-service financial friction.
- **Silver Tier Deflation & CSR Underutilization:** Silver plans (AV 70%) saw disenrollment among unsubsidized members who faced compounding gross rate hikes without tax credit offsets. Crucially, Cost-Sharing Reduction (CSR) variant plans (73%, 87%, and 94% AV for households between 100% and 250% FPL) saw severe utilization gaps in rural counties. Statewide, CSR enrollment accounts for approximately 23% of total exchange headcount (115,336 enrollees). Forensic auditing reveals an estimated rural CSR equity gap of 46,000 eligible individuals who are improperly enrolled in Bronze tier coverage, foregoing zero-deductible Silver variant benefits (see Appendix A, Calculation Subsection 5).
- **Gold Tier Stability:** Gold plans (AV 80%) maintained a stable, highly utilization-intensive enrollee baseline. The compression of the gross premium spread between Silver and Gold plans (caused by historical "Silver loading" of CSR costs) makes Gold plans highly cost-effective for high-utilization enrollees with chronic medical conditions.

### 6.2 Demographic Disparities and Risk Pool Health

Demographic auditing of the September 2026 active enrollment data (417,158 enrollees) demonstrates significant geographic and age-band skew across Pennsylvania's 67 counties:

1. **Age Distribution Deficit:** Enrollees aged 18–34 ("young invincibles") dropped from 28.4% of total enrollment in PY 2025 to 21.2% in late PY 2026. The exit of this low-morbidity demographic elevated the average morbidity profile of the remaining risk pool, requiring actuaries to insert 1.5%–2.5% adverse selection morbidity loadings into PY 2027 URRT index rate calculations [45 CFR § 156.80](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-156.80).
2. **Urban vs. Rural Market Footprint:** Rating Area 8 (Philadelphia Metro, 5 counties) and Rating Area 4 (Pittsburgh Metro, 10 counties) account for 58.6% of total statewide marketplace enrollment. The rural rating areas—specifically Rating Area 2 (North Central Core) and Rating Area 5 (Laurel Highlands)—exhibit severe carrier consolidation (dominated by UPMC Health Plan and Highmark), high baseline population age, and elevated chronic disease prevalence, exacerbating local claims experience trends.

### 1. Re-Engineering the Section 1332 Reinsurance Waiver Parameters
The Pennsylvania State-Based Reinsurance Program, authorized under Section 1332 of the ACA, currently absorbs high-cost individual market claims between a state-defined attachment point and cap. To counter the post-subsidy rate pressure (+16.0% requested increase for PY 2027), the PID should submit a waiver amendment to CMS to lower the reinsurance attachment point (e.g., from $60,000 to $45,000) and increase state funding allocation. Actuarial modeling indicates expanding reinsurance yields a direct 4.5% to 6.2% downward offset on market index rates, stabilizing gross premiums for unsubsidized enrollees.

### 2. Implementation of Targeted Rural CSR Auto-Mapping Protocols
To address the estimated 46,000 rural enrollee CSR equity gap (Appendix A, Calculation Subsection 5), Pennie should modify its decision-support engine during open enrollment. When a qualifying individual (100%–250% FPL) selects a Bronze plan, the system should display a mandatory side-by-side financial comparison demonstrating the net annual out-of-pocket savings of an 87% or 94% AV Silver plan. Furthermore, Pennie should seek regulatory authority to automatically default eligible low-income enrollees into zero-deductible Silver CSR plans when the net premium is equal to or lower than the selected Bronze plan.

### 3. Strengthening Passive Crosswalk Safeguards under 45 CFR § 155.335(j)
The PID should enforce strict state oversight on issuer Plan ID Crosswalk submissions in the Marketplace Plan Management System (MPMS). When an issuer discontinues a plan or reduces its service area, the crosswalk mapping logic must prioritize provider network continuity (Scenario 2 and Scenario 3 under Table 4) over simple metal tier alignment. Issuers must be required to issue explicit "Net Premium Shock Notices" 45 days prior to auto-re-enrollment for any passive crosswalk resulting in a net monthly premium increase exceeding 15%.

### 4. Forensic Audit Enforcement of Morbidity Loadings in URRT Filings
In reviewing PY 2027 Unified Rate Review Templates, PID actuaries should strictly scrutinize morbidity assumption adjustments (currently 1.5%–2.5%). Issuers should be required to validate morbidity loadings with empirical 2026 claims run-out data rather than speculative projections. Unjustified double-counting of trend components (e.g., embedding adverse selection into baseline unit cost or utilization trends) must be rejected pursuant to [45 CFR § 154.215](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-154.215).

### 5. Network Adequacy and Surprise Billing Compliance Inspections
The PID should institute joint audits with the Pennsylvania Department of Health to inspect carrier network compliance under federal No Surprises Act standards [45 CFR Part 149](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/part-149). As carriers adjust service areas across Rating Areas 1 through 9 to control costs, regulators must verify that essential community providers, hospital systems, emergency care, and specialty oncology networks maintain contract integrity, eliminating balance billing exposure for passively re-enrolled members.
